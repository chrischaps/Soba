# Synthesizer 101 with Soba, episode 1: How a Synthesizer Works (#477).
# The script, with narration and production notes, is the Claude Doc
# "Ep 1 · How a Synthesizer Works". It builds a synth voice from nothing, so
# it's presented over an empty patch, and kept as a take to film:
#
#   soba presentations/empty.json --present presentations/ep1-how-a-synth-works.txt --take ep1-take.txt
#   soba presentations/empty.json --capture ep1-take.txt --out target/showcase/ep1 --size 1920x1080 --ppp 1.5 --fps 30 --crf 12 --preset fast
#
# Present in a window 1280x720 points (1920x1080 at 150% scaling): the
# first module is placed at a point in the window, and the take keeps it
# there. Every later module is placed from the Oscillator's corner, in patch
# points, so the layout holds whatever the zoom. Where it ends up:
#
#   Note In  Oscillator  SVF Filter  VCA          Audio Output
#                                    ADSR Envelope
#
# A beat that ends where the narration says [LISTEN] is split in two, so
# the sound waits for your press.

title    How a Synthesizer Works
advance  cc 64                    # a sustain pedal plays the next beat
back     cc 65                    # ...and a second one, if there is one, goes back

beat Cold open
say   Every sound you just heard started as **one plain tone** that never stops.
say   In the next twelve minutes, we'll build this synthesizer from nothing, and you'll know what every one of these cables is doing.
0     play                       # Soba opens stopped
0     aim 0 0 1.0                # a known view, so the first module lands where the take expects

beat The idea
say   Think about your own voice. Your vocal cords **buzz**. On their own, they'd sound like a kazoo. Your **mouth** shapes that buzz, darker or brighter, into vowels. Your **breath** decides when a sound starts, how it swells, and how it fades. And you choose which **notes** to sing.
say   A synthesizer does the same four jobs. An oscillator buzzes. A filter is the mouth. An envelope and an amplifier are the breath. And a keyboard chooses the notes.
say   On most synths, that wiring is hidden inside the box. Soba is a **modular** synth: every job is a separate module, and you join them with cables, so you can watch each one work. Let's build one, a job at a time.
say   And you can build it **with me**. Soba runs in your browser at soba.chaps.dev, with nothing to install. Open it, press New in the toolbar for an empty canvas, and pause the video whenever you want to catch up.

# --- Step 1: A buzz ---------------------------------------------------------

beat A source
say   Every sound starts at a **source**. Press Space and type "osc", or right-click the canvas and choose Source, Oscillator.
0     cursor on
0     move 420 300 0.8

beat An oscillator
say   An oscillator makes a tone by repeating one wave shape, over and over, hundreds of times a second.
say   To hear it, it needs a way out to your speakers. Add an **Audio Output**, and turn your volume down a little first.
0     key Space 0.05
0.3   type osc 0.12
1.2   key Enter 0.05
1.6   look osc.sine 1.4

beat An output
say   Now the first cable: from the oscillator's Out, on its right side, to the output's Mono.
0     aim 230 150 0.62 1.4
1.6   move osc.sine 898 0 0.8
2.6   key Space 0.05
2.9   type output 0.12
3.8   key Enter 0.05
4     param output.audio Volume 0.3   # a raw saw is loud: the drone stays gentle

beat The first cable
say   Signals always come out of a module's right side, and go into another's left.
say   [LISTEN]
say   That's a sine wave, the purest tone there is. And look at the cable. It's **blue**, because it carries sound. Blue cables are audio.
say   Now the buzz. Set Wave to **Saw**.
0     move osc.sine out:Out 0.8
1     drag output.audio in:Mono 1.2
2.4   move off

beat Saw
say   [LISTEN]
say   Brighter, and buzzier. A saw is packed with extra frequencies stacked above the note, called **harmonics**. They're what the filter will carve, later on, and Episode 2 is all about them.
say   But listen to what this really is: one note. It doesn't start, it doesn't stop, and it never changes. An oscillator knows nothing about music. Everything from here is about telling it what to do.
say   To play notes, we need something to choose them. Add a **Note In**, from the Source list.
0     look osc.sine 1.0
0.6   move osc.sine knob:Waveform 0.7
1.6   param osc.sine Waveform 1
2.6   move off
3     aim 230 150 0.62 2.0

# --- Step 2: Notes ----------------------------------------------------------

beat Note In
say   It plays from a MIDI keyboard, from the piano on its face, or from your computer's keyboard.
say   Note In can play chords, but the synth we're building plays **one note at a time**, like the classic ones. So turn its Voices knob down to one.
0     move osc.sine -232 20 0.8
1     key Space 0.05
1.3   type note 0.12
2.2   key Enter 0.05
2.6   aim -247 200 0.95 1.4

beat One voice
say   Its **Pitch** output goes into the oscillator's first input, V/Oct, short for **volts per octave**.
0     move input.poly_midi knob:Voices 0.7
1     param input.poly_midi Voices 1 0.8   # 8 voices on one oscillator stack up 6 dB louder
2.2   move off

beat Pitch
say   Old synths set pitch with a voltage: one volt up, one octave up. Episode 3 is all about it. For now, V/Oct just means pitch.
say   This cable is **orange**. Orange cables are control: they don't make sound, they move something, like an invisible hand on a knob. Play the bottom row of your keyboard. Z is C, X is D, and on up the scale.
0     move input.poly_midi out:Pitch 0.8
1     drag osc.sine in:V/Oct 1.2
2.4   move off

beat Pitch · play

say   It follows along. But let go, and the sound keeps going: the oscillator never stops. We can choose the note now, but not when it starts or ends.
say   For that, we need two things: a signal that says when a key is down, and something that can turn the sound up and down.
say   The second is a **VCA**, a voltage-controlled amplifier. In plain words, it's a volume knob that a cable can turn. It goes between the oscillator and the output.

beat A VCA · listen
say   [LISTEN]
say   Nothing changed. With nothing in its CV input, a VCA lets everything through at full volume.
say   Now Note In's **Gate**. It's **green**: green cables are gates, on while a key is held, and off when you let go.
0     note 60 80 1.5

beat The gate
say   The VCA's CV input is orange, and Soba lets the gate in anyway. A gate is just a number, zero or one, and a volume knob understands those perfectly: silent, and full.
0     move input.poly_midi out:Gate 0.8
1     drag util.vca in:CV 1.6
2.8   move off
3     look close util.vca 1.6

beat The gate · play
say   [LISTEN]
say   Notes! Each one starts when you press, and stops when you let go. But listen to how: each note slams on and off, like a light switch. Real instruments don't. A plucked string rings and fades; a voice can swell. We need **breath**.
say   Breath is the job of an **envelope**. Add an ADSR Envelope, from the Modulation list.
0     aim 230 150 0.62 1.2
0.6   note 60 80 0.2
1     note 64 80 0.2

# --- Step 3: Breath ---------------------------------------------------------

beat An envelope
say   Its first input is a green Gate. Just to see, let's try the Pitch output there.
0     param output.audio Volume 0.5 1.0   # up while the VCA is shut: notes from here on come and go
0     move osc.sine 594 300 0.8
1     key Space 0.05
1.3   type env 0.12
2.2   key Enter 0.05
2.6   aim 0 220 0.72 1.4

beat A guard rail
say   It won't stay. A gate input needs a clean on or off, and pitch could be any number at all, so Soba won't let it in. Most crossings are fair game; this is one of the few **guard rails**.
say   So the Gate goes in instead. One output can feed as many inputs as you like.
0     move input.poly_midi out:Pitch 0.8
1     drag mod.adsr in:Gate 1.6
3.2   move off

beat The shape
say   Then the envelope's Out goes into the VCA's CV, in place of the gate.
0     move input.poly_midi out:Gate 0.8
1     drag mod.adsr in:Gate 1.6
3     move mod.adsr out:Out 0.8
4     drag util.vca in:CV 1.2
5.4   move off
5.6   aim 489 270 0.8 1.6

beat The shape · play
say   [LISTEN]
say   Now when a key goes down, the envelope draws a **shape**, and that shape turns the volume for us. Its four stages are its name. **Attack** is how long it takes to rise. **Decay** is how long it then takes to fall. **Sustain** is the level it holds while you keep the key down: a level, not a time. And **Release** is how long it fades after you let go.
say   Sustain all the way down, and a short decay.
0     note 60 76 1.0
2     note 64 76 1.0
4     note 67 76 1.0

beat When into how
say   And look at what the envelope did with the colours: a green gate went in, and an orange shape came out. An envelope turns **when** into **how**. Episode 5 is all about it.
say   The last job is the mouth: shaping the buzz. That's a **filter**. Add an SVF Filter, from the Filter list, between the oscillator and the VCA.
0     param mod.adsr Attack 0.005 1.2
0     param mod.adsr Decay 0.2 1.2

# --- Step 4: A mouth --------------------------------------------------------

beat A filter
say   The oscillator goes into its In, and its LowPass output into the VCA.
0     aim 230 150 0.62 1.4
1.6   move osc.sine 292 0 0.8

7.2   move filter.svf out:LowPass 0.8
8.2   drag util.vca in:In 1.2
9.6   move off

beat Cutoff
say   A low-pass filter lets the low frequencies through and takes the high ones away, like a hand over your mouth. **Cutoff** is where it starts cutting. Hold a note, and bring it down.
0     look filter.svf 1.2
0.6   move filter.svf knob:Cutoff 0.7

beat Cutoff · listen
say   [LISTEN]
say   Down, and the buzz goes dark and muffled. Up, and the brightness comes back. The display shows you which harmonics are getting through.
say   The next knob, **Resonance**, boosts the frequencies right at the cutoff. Turn it up, and sweep again.
0     note 48 76 10.4
0.6   param filter.svf Cutoff 150 3.0
4.2   param filter.svf Cutoff 8000 3.6
8.4   param filter.svf Cutoff 1000 1.6
10.6  move off

beat Resonance
say   [LISTEN]
say   That vocal, almost talking sound is the filter starting to **sing**. Episode 4 is all about it. Let's bring it back down.
say   Here's the move that brings it to life. Voices and instruments are brightest at the start of a note, and mellow as it rings. So give the envelope a second job. Its Out goes into the filter's **Cutoff** input as well.
0     param filter.svf Cutoff 150 0.6     # closed first, so the note starts soft
0     move filter.svf knob:Resonance 0.7
1     param filter.svf Resonance 0.8 1.2
2.6   note 48 68 9.0
2.9   move filter.svf knob:Cutoff 0.6
3.2   param filter.svf Cutoff 2400 4.4
7.8   param filter.svf Cutoff 300 3.4
11.8  param filter.svf Resonance 0.3 1.0
11.8  param filter.svf Cutoff 1000 1.0
13    move off
